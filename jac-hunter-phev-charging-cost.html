Hunter charging: what could your week cost?

Home charging is where Hunter’s PHEV value can become a daily benefit. A predictable local route and a suitable overnight charging setup let you make use of the battery regularly. These worked examples show how to budget for that electricity without treating brochure range as a guaranteed result.

1) What Hunter offers for charging

The Australian Hunter has a 31.2kWh battery. JAC lists 11kW AC charging and up to 46kW DC charging, with indicative times of 3.5 hours from 15–100% on AC and 30 minutes from 15–80% on DC. The opportunity is to refill while the ute is already parked; faster charging is useful when the available stop is shorter.

Actual time depends on the charger, electrical supply, battery and conditions. An 11kW-capable vehicle does not make every home installation deliver 11kW. Have an electrician assess the intended parking location and supply. JAC charging specifications.

2) One charging session at three electricity prices

For a simple session budget, assume the charger records 30kWh delivered from the wall. This is a chosen example, not Hunter’s usable battery capacity or a guaranteed full-charge requirement. The meter reading includes energy used during charging; the amount stored in the battery can be lower.

3) A 50km weekday example

Assume five 50km days per week, 48 weeks per year: 12,000km. If all those kilometres are electric, and measured wall electricity averages an assumed 30kWh/100km, the annual requirement is 3,600kWh. This consumption assumption includes charging losses and is not a tested Hunter result.

4) Why regular charging matters more than the best-case fuel label

The positive case is simple: if your route and charging routine let you replace petrol kilometres with reasonably priced electricity, the plug-in hardware is doing useful work. Hunter’s combination of an electric-driving option and a petrol engine also gives flexibility for days that exceed your normal local route.

Do not assume the engine will stay off for every kilometre in the example. Load, temperature, battery state, driving mode and power demand can change operation. For a mixed week, add electricity for the electric kilometres and petrol for the engine-powered kilometres. Do not add charging cost to a fuel-only brochure figure and call the result a real-world test.

5) Build your own budget

  1. Read your actual electricity tariff and any charging time restrictions.
  2. Record a normal week’s distance and realistic opportunities to plug in.
  3. After delivery, use charger meter readings and fuel receipts to replace the assumptions.
  4. Add installation, insurance, servicing, finance and depreciation for a full ownership comparison.

Our assessment: Hunter is especially worth considering for local drivers with dependable charging. Use the five-year ownership calculator for a wider budget, and the charging guide for setup questions.
