Why Hunter is a strong value choice

Yes—on its published Australian specification and launch pricing, Hunter makes a strong value case for buyers who can charge regularly and want a ute for more than commuting. Its appeal is the combination: useful electric driving, substantial towing capability and a choice of equipment levels without a different core powertrain.

1) The entry grade keeps the important hardware

The most persuasive part of Hunter’s value story is what the Pro does not make you upgrade to obtain. Both pickups share the same 360kW system, 31.2kWh battery and 3500kg braked tow rating. That makes the lower-priced grade a serious candidate for someone who wants the plug-in drivetrain and ute capability first.

We see that as a useful buying advantage: a buyer can prioritise the vehicle’s main job before spending on comfort or appearance. Those published specifications are a reason to book a test drive, rather than evidence of measured acceleration, towing economy or long-term durability. JAC Australian range and equipment.

2) A clear starting point for the budget

Reported Australian launch list prices are $49,988 for Pro and $54,844 for X, before on-road costs. The $4,856 step between them creates a straightforward choice: keep the budget for charging and your actual ute setup, or buy X’s extra convenience and off-road equipment.

Use those launch prices as a reference, then request an itemised quote including registration, stamp duty, dealer delivery, paint and accessories. The entry list price is below $50,000; that does not make it a sub-$50,000 driveaway ute. Published launch pricing, 10 July 2026.

4) Safety adds substance to the shortlist

A low purchase price is much more convincing when safety information is available. Hunter has a five-star ANCAP rating with a 2024 datestamp. The assessment draws on T9 results plus additional Hunter tests, rather than simply assuming that the diesel ute’s rating transfers unchanged.

For family buyers, read the applicability and child-restraint guidance: ANCAP advises against a restraint in the rear centre position because there is no top tether anchorage. Hunter ANCAP assessment.

5) Our buying recommendation

Put Hunter high on your test-drive list if you have reliable charging, want a capable pickup and care about the equipment you get for your money. Pro is our starting point for value; X is the next step when you will use its added features.

Before committing, price insurance, inspect the service arrangements near you and try the vehicle with your normal passengers and parking routine. Long-term resale and reliability are not yet established. We would reassess the value case for a buyer who rarely charges or routinely tows close to the maximum combined mass. This is an editorial recommendation based on published information, not a scored ranking or our own road test.
