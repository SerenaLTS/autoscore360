One PHEV ute for work, family and weekends

A PHEV ute is most convincing when it can cover several jobs in your normal week: local work trips, family transport and weekend cargo. Hunter deserves a prominent place on that shortlist because its plug-in drivetrain comes with pickup practicality and a useful choice between value-focused Pro and better-equipped X.

Map a normal week before choosing a grade: who travels, what stays in the tub, where the vehicle charges and how often it tows. Hunter looks strongest when those needs overlap.

This turns “is a PHEV good?” into a more useful question: will you use both its charging capability and its ute body often enough to justify the purchase? A vehicle that fits Monday through Friday is easier to live with than one chosen only for an occasional holiday.

2) Why Hunter is a compelling starting point

Hunter’s appeal for mixed use is that the entry Pro grade retains the main PHEV hardware and towing rating. Buyers can choose the equipment level around their routine instead of stepping up solely for the stronger drivetrain. For a family sharing the vehicle, X’s 360-degree camera and heated front seats may make its extra cost easier to justify.

The pickup also includes useful cargo features such as a bed liner, tub lighting and tailgate lift assistance. These are practical benefits to inspect at a dealer: try the tailgate, check how you will secure work gear and see whether your usual bags or equipment fit. 3) Make the family check specific

Hunter’s five-star ANCAP result is a solid reason to investigate further, but seating fit is personal. Bring your own child restraints and check the approved positions, installation and front-seat space. ANCAP notes that the rear centre seat has no top tether anchorage and does not recommend child-restraint installation there. ANCAP family-relevant safety details.

Test entry and exit for the passengers who will use the vehicle, then park it somewhere representative of home or school. Check visibility, turning space and access to the tub with the vehicle parked. These checks are more useful than assuming a large vehicle will automatically suit every family.

4) Keep work equipment in the load calculation

Tools, a canopy and a towbar often stay fitted when the work ute becomes the family car. Add those items before passengers, luggage and trailer towball load. Hunter’s published 915kg nominal payload is a starting point, not a spare allowance after accessories.

For towing, the 6430kg GCM matters as well as the 3500kg braked tow rating. Use the worked Hunter towing example and load calculator before deciding how much equipment can stay aboard. Staying comfortably inside the limits is part of choosing the right setup.

5) Take this shortlist to the test drive

  • Pro: start here if the budget, core drivetrain and useful cargo space lead your decision.
  • X: compare it when parking assistance, heated seats and differential locks are features you actually want.
  • Charging: arrange a realistic assessment of home or workplace supply.
  • Support: check service access, warranty conditions and an itemised quote.
Our recommendation: Hunter is a strong candidate for a household that needs a working pickup and can charge regularly. The best version is the one that fits your passengers, load and budget—not simply the highest specification.
