Australian light-truck licence guide

Car licence trucks in Australia

A car licence generally limits the truck to no more than 4.5 tonnes GVM—but 4.5 tonnes is not payload. The body, fuel, driver, passengers, tools and accessories all consume the rated mass before cargo is added.

Reviewed 31 August 2026. Driver licensing is administered by states and territories. Verify the vehicle's compliance plate, registration details, transmission conditions and your licence with the relevant road authority before driving.

What can you drive on a car licence?

Across Australia, a rigid vehicle at or below 4.5 tonnes gross vehicle mass is the common car-licence boundary. A vehicle above 4.5 tonnes GVM is a heavy vehicle under the Heavy Vehicle National Law and normally requires the appropriate heavy-vehicle licence class.

GVM

Gross Vehicle Mass is the maximum permitted mass of the loaded truck as rated. It includes the vehicle, body, fuel, occupants, accessories and load.

Tare or unladen mass

The truck's empty mass depends on how it is measured and what is installed. A cab-chassis tare is not the same as a completed tray, tipper or pantech tare.

Payload

A practical starting calculation is registered GVM minus the completed operating mass. Axle limits and weight distribution can reduce usable payload further.

A model capable of a higher manufacturer's GVM is not automatically car-licence legal. It must be correctly rated and registered at no more than the applicable limit; do not rely on driving it lightly loaded.

4.5t car licence or LR licence?

Stay at 4.5t when

The measured operating load fits comfortably, several car-licence drivers must share the vehicle, and the business can control overloading.

Investigate LR when

The body and normal load leave too little margin, load varies, towing is important, or a higher registered GVM materially improves legal payload.

Do not solve payload with towing alone

GCM, towing capacity, trailer ATM/GTM, towbar rating, licence conditions and load distribution all apply separately.

Plan driver competency

Legal entitlement is only the baseline. Truck width, height, braking, mirrors, tail swing, load restraint and fatigue require training.

Checks before buying a car-licence truck

  • Ask for the exact registered GVM and a copy of the compliance information.
  • Obtain completed tare and front/rear axle estimates with the actual body and accessories.
  • Add driver, passengers, fuel, tools, restraints, tow equipment and the heaviest normal load.
  • Allow operating margin rather than planning to sit exactly at 4,500kg.
  • Check height, width, parking, loading-zone and route access for the completed truck.
  • Confirm insurance, driver training, load restraint and workplace procedures.
  • Weigh the completed vehicle and representative load; keep the result with fleet records.
