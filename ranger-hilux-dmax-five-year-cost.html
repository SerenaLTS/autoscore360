Fair comparison

Start with equivalent vehicles

Do not compare a four-cylinder Ranger XLT against a discounted HiLux Rogue and a D-MAX X-Terrain, then attribute the entire difference to the badge. Match body, drivetrain, safety equipment, tow hardware and the features you would actually buy.

Where each can win

Three different ownership propositions

Ford Ranger

Cost strength: broad range lets buyers select the powertrain and equipment they need; resale demand has been supported by high market visibility.
Risk: a V6 or premium grade raises purchase, insurance and tyre exposure.

Toyota HiLux

Cost strength: established resale and service familiarity can support the long-term equation.
Risk: paying a large “Toyota premium” upfront can erase a later resale advantage.

Isuzu D-MAX

Cost strength: work-focused 3.0-litre proposition and long warranty offer under applicable conditions.
Risk: transaction price, insurance and future resale still vary by grade and market conditions.

Sensitivity

Resale often dominates the fuel difference

At 20,000km per year and $2.00 per litre, a 0.5L/100km consumption difference equals about $200 a year, or $1,000 over five years before price changes. On a $65,000 vehicle, a five-percentage-point difference in retained value equals $3,250. This is why a precise-looking total built on one resale forecast can be misleading.

Optimistic case

Strong resale, no major out-of-warranty repair, lower fuel price and a competitive dealer purchase.

Conservative case

Weaker resale, higher fuel and insurance, more tyres and a realistic repair contingency.

Our buying rule

Do not pay more than the benefit is worth to you

If Ranger is $8,000 more after matching equipment, decide whether its performance, cabin and towing technology are worth that amount before assuming resale will recover it. If HiLux costs more, model the retained-value uplift required to break even. If D-MAX is discounted, check whether the comfort and technology trade-offs matter across your real kilometres.
