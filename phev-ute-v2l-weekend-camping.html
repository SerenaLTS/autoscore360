A Weekend Camping With a PHEV Ute: Plan Your V2L Power

A PHEV ute can be useful at camp as well as on the road. V2L adds a way to power compatible appliances from the vehicle, making Hunter worth considering for buyers who want one pickup for the trip and the campsite. The right starting point is a small appliance plan, not the battery’s headline size.

Choose the jobs that actually need electricity

Start with the fridge, lighting and devices you will use over the weekend. Write down each appliance’s input power and expected operating time. Include time between arrival and departure, not simply the hours you are awake. A compressor fridge’s label rating and its average use over a day are different things.

There is a practical appeal to having a power source already travelling with the ute. It may reduce the need for a separate solution for some tasks, depending on your equipment and reserve requirements. It does not mean that every owner can leave all other camping power equipment at home.

Energy capacity and output power answer different questions

A weekend energy total tells you how much energy the appliances use over time. The output rating tells you what can run at one moment. A kettle, cooking appliance or motor starting up may demand much more power than the small loads in the table. Check continuous demand, start-up surge and the approved connection arrangement before deciding which appliances can operate together.

JAC lists V2L on both Hunter pickup grades, but the linked Australian specification table marks it as included without stating a numeric output rating. We therefore do not assign Hunter an unverified kW limit. Ask for the approved adapter, operating limits and demonstration for the exact vehicle. This keeps a useful feature grounded in what you can actually connect.

Protect the energy you need after camp

Choose a reserve based on the next drive, available charging and the vehicle’s operating rules. Ask how V2L stops at its minimum battery threshold and how the system behaves at low charge. Do not assume that the whole advertised battery capacity is available to external appliances.

If the vehicle has to leave camp during the weekend, consider what happens to equipment such as the fridge while it is away. A separate small battery may still be convenient for continuous loads. V2L is an extra option in the camping plan, not a requirement to make everything depend on the ute.

The Hunter buying case

Hunter is a strong candidate when you want a pickup that supports plug-in local driving and offers a practical mobile-power function for occasional trips. Pro keeps V2L in the lower grade; compare X if its extra equipment also suits your daily life. Test the setup before the first trip rather than learning the controls at a remote campsite.

Follow the vehicle and adapter instructions for weather protection, ventilation, load limits and connection. V2L is not permission to feed power into household wiring. Keep the campsite plan to approved appliance use, and read the broader V2L guide for other work and recreation scenarios.
