If we could buy only one

Our conditional pick: Ford Ranger XLT

For a buyer who has not supplied a specific budget or duty cycle, Ranger XLT is the least compromised place to begin. It combines a usable family cabin, established towing aids, broad accessory support and a choice of diesel powertrains. Ford lists up to 3,500kg towing for properly equipped mainstream Rangers, but the actual payload and combination limit still vary by configuration.

Why it wins the default question

It covers work, family, towing and touring without forcing the buyer into a specialist grade. The V6 adds performance and full-time 4WD availability; the four-cylinder can reduce purchase and running cost.

Why it may not win your question

It can cost more than a Triton or value-led entrant, and a buyer prioritising long-term simplicity, remote-area familiarity or daily electric driving may rationally choose something else.

Decision order

Ask these questions before comparing scores

1. What is the heaviest real load?

Add people, accessories, canopy, tools, luggage and towball download. Space remaining in the tub is not legal payload remaining.

2. Can a PHEV be charged routinely?

If not, do not buy from the headline combined fuel figure. Model petrol use with the battery depleted.

3. Where will it be serviced?

A warranty is most useful when an appropriate dealer is accessible. Regional and remote buyers should map support before buying.

4. How long will it be kept?

Short ownership makes transaction price and resale more important; long ownership increases the value of service history, warranty conditions and repair access.
