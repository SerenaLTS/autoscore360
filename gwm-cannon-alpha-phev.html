GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV: the big-battery PHEV ute play

The Cannon Alpha PHEV makes a strong numbers case: large battery, long claimed electric range, 300kW/750Nm output and 3500kg braked towing. The catch is payload, offer variability and the usual question of how Chinese-brand resale and support mature over time.

If your PHEV ute shortlist starts with electric range and 3500kg towing, Cannon Alpha PHEV deserves attention. If payload is king, read the numbers twice and recheck current driveaway offers before comparing value.

Verdict

1) What makes it different

GWM's pitch is simple: electrification without giving up the headline ute capability signals. Its battery is much larger than Ford's and its claimed EV range is longer than BYD's WLTP Performance figure, though GWM uses NEDC claims on the local page. Current award and offer claims can help the showroom story, but they should be treated as time-sensitive rather than permanent specs.

Best read: Cannon Alpha PHEV is the spec-sheet extrovert: power, battery size, EV range and towing all look strong. Payload is the number that prevents a simple win.

Payload guide

Payload behind the 3500kg tow claim

The published 685kg payload is the practical constraint behind the strong towing headline. Battery mass, passengers, accessories and trailer towball download all consume the allowance, so a 3500kg tow rating does not mean every fully loaded touring setup will work.

  • Add occupants, luggage and every fitted accessory.
  • Add the trailer's actual towball download.
  • Check payload, GVM, GCM, axle and towbar limits independently.
  • Use a weighbridge for the finished setup and verify the exact grade.
