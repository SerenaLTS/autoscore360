SITRAK G5S vs G7S vs C9H: start with the work

Two trucks can look similar on a specification sheet and fit very different working days. A tight delivery yard, a heavy body or a regular climb can change the shortlist. This guide looks at where G5S, G7S and C9H fit, and what to compare alongside them.

G7S or C9H for prime-mover work?

Use the G7S specification guide and C9H specification guide as starting points. Ask for proposals covering the same trailer, route and operating mass. The higher-output option needs to justify its purchase and operating cost on your work.

Regional and repeat-route freight

Provide a representative route, loading pattern, typical mass, depot access and daily hours. Ask the dealer to explain the proposed driveline and how the truck will be serviced between shifts.

Long-distance and demanding routes

Add gradients, expected time at high load, overnight accommodation needs and workshop access away from base. A route assessment is more useful than choosing on peak power alone.

Request a demonstration in a configuration representative of your work where available. An unloaded drive can help assess controls and visibility, but cannot establish loaded fuel consumption or hill performance.

For a tipper, mixer or hooklift, buy the completed job

Give the body builder and dealer the same brief. Confirm who coordinates chassis preparation, PTO, hydraulics, electrical interfaces, body mounting, final weighing and handover. Ask for the finished dimensions and a mass estimate showing fuel, body, equipment and the intended load.

A chassis mass rating is not a promised payload. Manufacturer ratings also do not establish route access or permissible operating mass. Have the proposed configuration and operating route checked against the applicable requirements before committing.

Divide expected annual ownership and operating cost by productive kilometres, operating hours or delivered loads—whichever matches the contract. Show how the result changes if fuel or downtime is higher than expected.

A quote brief you can copy

  • Application and proposed body or trailer.
  • Typical and maximum load; expected axle layout.
  • Routes, gradients, depot access and site surface.
  • Annual kilometres, daily hours and shift pattern.
  • PTO, hydraulics, safety equipment and cab requirements.
  • Delivery deadline, body-build responsibility and handover requirements.
  • Service location and parts/support expectations.

Send the same brief to two or three suppliers.
